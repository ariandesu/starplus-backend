HOW THIS DATA WAS GENERATED
astronaut_health_SYNTHETIC.csv
Created 2026-11-06

=====================================================================
1. READ THIS FIRST: THE DATA IS SYNTHETIC, NOT REAL ASTRONAUT DATA
=====================================================================
Every row was generated by a computer program. No row is a measurement
from a real person, and no row came from NASA or any other space agency.
The astronaut IDs (SYN-A001 and so on) are invented and do not map to
real people. The "agency" column is a randomly assigned label.

Do not describe this file as NASA, agency or real astronaut data. In a
report, thesis or paper, describe it as synthetic data based on published
group-level statistics.

=====================================================================
2. WHY REAL DATA WAS NOT USED
=====================================================================
The request was 1000+ real rows covering all the listed measures, with a
sick/fit label and no missing values. I could not find such a dataset:

- Individual astronaut medical data is protected and small in number.
  Astronauts are few and easy to identify, so these records are held
  under controlled access.
- The only record-level public data for these measures found in the
  earlier project in this folder (STAR_PLUS_ASTRONAUT_DATASET) is 28
  serum glucose values from 4 Inspiration4 crew (NASA OSDR, OSD-575).
- Published studies give group averages, not rows per person.
- No sick/fit label was found in any source. It would have to be created
  by someone in any case.

Limits of this check: general web search was failing in this session
(a tool error), so no new sweep of dataset repositories was done. The
availability conclusion relies on the earlier project's report, plus
targeted paper lookups through Europe PMC and PubMed. Access terms were
not re-checked today.

Routes to real data (all need an application and approval):
- NASA Life Sciences Data Archive / LSAH:
  https://lsda.jsc.nasa.gov/explore/lsdahome/datarequest
- TRISH EXPAND database (commercial crews):
  https://bcm.edu/academic-centers/space-medicine/expand
Public, but glucose only: https://osdr.nasa.gov/bio/repo/data/studies/OSD-575

=====================================================================
3. HOW THE ROWS WERE BUILT
=====================================================================
Program: generate_synthetic_dataset.py (random seed 20261106). Running it
again gives a byte-identical CSV.

1. Create 180 synthetic astronauts, each with their own baseline values.
2. Assign an agency label at random. Weights are my rough choice: NASA
   45%, Roscosmos 20%, ESA 10%, CNSA 10%, JAXA 5%, CSA 5%, ISRO 3%,
   MBRSC 2%. Physiology is not modelled by agency.
3. Create 7 records per astronaut, one per mission phase: L-90, L-10,
   in-flight day 21, 75 and 150, R+7, R+60 (L = days before launch,
   R = days after return). Phase effects from section 4 are applied.
4. Decide sick (1) or fit (0) for each record, using a phase-specific
   probability and a per-astronaut tendency to be ill.
5. For sick records, pick one of 6 conditions: infection, dehydration,
   orthostatic intolerance, sleep/behavioral, metabolic, hypertensive.
   Apply its effects at a random severity (0.35 to 1.3), so that mild
   cases overlap with fit records. The condition is not saved in the CSV.
6. Add random noise, limit values to plausible ranges, and round.

Links built in: short sleep raises stress, stress lowers mood, and short
sleep lowers sleep quality. These directions are consistent with the 2022
Sleep paper in section 4; the sizes of the effects are my assumptions.

=====================================================================
4. WHERE EACH COLUMN'S NUMBERS CAME FROM
=====================================================================
[P] = target taken from a published or public astronaut source
[A] = my assumption (general physiology or a chosen scale), NOT astronaut
      data and not checked against a source

[P] systolic_bp_mmHg: 120 before flight, falling to 106 by about 6 months
    in flight (ref 1).
[P/A] diastolic_bp_mmHg: in-flight drop of 9 from ref 2. The baseline of
    76 is [A].
[P] mean_arterial_pressure_mmHg: calculated as (SBP + 2 x DBP) / 3. Not
    an independent measure. Ref 2 reports a 10 mmHg in-flight drop.
[P] blood_glucose_mg_dL: mean 80.5, SD 11.1, calculated from the real
    OSD-575 values (ref 3), leaving out the 4 R+45 values. Three of those
    are unexplained lows (36-44). All 28 values give mean 76.4, SD 16.2.
    This is 4 people on a 3-day mission, so it is a weak basis.
[P/A] core_body_temp_C: rise of about 1 C developing over 2.5 months in
    flight (ref 4). The 37.0 C baseline is [A].
[P/A] heart_rate_bpm: no change in flight (ref 2). The 65 bpm baseline
    is [A].
[A] hrv_rmssd_ms: baseline of 45 ms. No usable published value was
    extracted.
[A] spo2_percent: baseline 97.6. No astronaut source found.
[A] respiratory_rate_bpm: baseline 14. No astronaut source used.
[P] sleep_duration_h: L-90 6.41, L-10 5.86, in flight 6.09, R+7 6.95
    (ref 5, ISS crew). R+60 reuses the L-90 value [A]. Ref 6 reports about
    6.5 h self-reported in flight; the actigraphy figures were used.
[A] sleep_quality_score_0_100: invented scale.
[A] mood_score_0_100: invented scale, not a validated questionnaire.
[A] physical_activity_min_per_day: minutes per day by phase.
[P/A] body_mass_kg: loss of 1.2 kg at about 3 months in flight (ref 7).
    The 76 kg baseline is [A].
    body_weight_lb: body_mass_kg x 2.20462, the same information in other
    units. Body mass and weight were both requested, so both are included.
[A] hydration_urine_specific_gravity: baseline 1.017. No astronaut
    source found.
[A] stress_score_0_100: invented scale, not a validated questionnaire.
    The link to short sleep follows ref 6 in direction only.
[A] sick/fit: 1 = sick, 0 = fit. See section 5.

Not modelled: age, sex, mission length and agency differences.

=====================================================================
5. THE sick/fit LABEL IS DEFINED BY MY RULES
=====================================================================
It is not a medical diagnosis and comes from no source. The 26.5% sick
rate was chosen so that both classes have enough rows. It is NOT a real
illness rate. For context, one ISS study reported 3.40 clinical events per
flight year and 46% of crew with a "notable" event (ref 8), but that does
not convert into a per-record rate. The phase probabilities, conditions
and effect sizes are all my assumptions.

=====================================================================
7. LIMITS IF YOU USE THIS FOR MACHINE LEARNING
=====================================================================
- A logistic regression scored ROC AUC 0.83 to 0.95 (5-fold, split by
  astronaut). This only shows a model can recover the rules in section 3.
  It says nothing about predicting illness in real astronauts, so do not
  report it as a clinical result.
- Each astronaut has 7 rows. Split train/test by astronaut_id, or the
  scores will be inflated.
- Drop mean_arterial_pressure_mmHg or body_weight_lb if you need
  independent features; they are derived from other columns.
- Do not compare agencies. Differences such as MBRSC's 43% sick rate come
  from chance and very small groups (3 synthetic astronauts).
- Matching published averages does not make individual rows or the
  relationships between columns realistic.
